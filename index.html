Razorpay partners with Namify to put a custom domain behind every payment link

Priya Anand — 5:30 PM IST · September 24, 2026
Image Credits: Getty Images

Razorpay, India's largest payments aggregator, has partnered with Namify, a domain registration provider, to let merchants attach a custom domain to their payment links and pages. The feature is rolling out across Razorpay's base of over 12 million merchants, with API access for larger platforms that want to build the flow directly into their own onboarding.

Razorpay built its payment links and pages to be a website substitute. Freelancers, coaches, and small sellers have used rzp.io links to get paid for years, often shared straight from Instagram bios or WhatsApp broadcasts. Razorpay's own marketing calls them a "zero-code storefront." The Namify integration is the next step: instead of a generic rzp.io link, sellers can now put their own domain on it. No rebuild, no migration. The old link keeps working exactly as it did.

As part of the launch, qualifying merchants also get a free one-year domain on extensions like .store and .online through Namify, with discounted renewal pricing after that.

The strategic play for Razorpay. Payment links already get sellers in the door at the bottom of the funnel. A branded domain gives Razorpay a second monetizable layer on top of that same relationship without touching the core payments business. It's incremental ARR on infrastructure that already exists, sold to a base that's already there.

It's also a retention lever. Once a seller's brand identity is tied to a Razorpay-hosted domain, the switching cost to move to a full website builder goes up. A rzp.io link was always a soft ceiling: sellers who wanted to look established had a reason to eventually graduate to Shopify or a dedicated site builder. A domain of their own removes that reason to leave, at least for the sellers who were never going to build a full site anyway.

The market-expansion angle. The bigger strategic bet is on total addressable market. Payment links have historically been seen as a stopgap, something a seller uses before they have a "real" online presence. India has over 63 million MSMEs, and millions of new small businesses come online each year. A large share of them default to website builders specifically because they associate a real domain with legitimacy, not because they need the extra features a full site provides.

By closing the domain gap, Razorpay is repositioning payment pages as a genuine end state rather than a placeholder, opening up a segment of sellers who were previously funneling straight into website-builder territory. For Namify, it's a distribution channel into first-time domain buyers who'd otherwise never have registered one on their own.

"Sellers shouldn't need a website to look like they have one," said Anand Lakshmanan at Razorpay. "Now they don't."

Namify domains connected through the integration resolve directly to the seller's existing Razorpay page, requiring no separate hosting or technical setup. Early interest has come from sellers, coaches, small D2C brands, and home businesses: the exact segment that would otherwise never have joined Razorpay at all, assuming a payment link was too technical or too generic to represent their brand. For Razorpay, that's net-new users it wasn't reaching before.
